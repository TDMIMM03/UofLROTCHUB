The RECONDO Badge at Advanced Camp

RECONDO is awarded to cadets who display physical superiority and first-attempt perfection across all CST evaluations.

RECONDO Qualification Criteria (Policy Memo #20):
  • AFT Physical Fitness: Achieve a PASS and high score on initial attempt during AFT.
  • Confidence Course: Successfully pass ALL 10 obstacles on the Confidence Course on initial attempt (6.0 pts).
  • 6-Mile Foot March Standard: Complete the 6-Mile Foot March in under 90 minutes (1 hr 30 mins) on initial attempt.
  • 12-Mile Foot March Standard: Complete the 12-Mile Foot March within prescribed time standards (3 hours max) on initial attempt.
  • Marksmanship Excellence: Qualify as EXPERT on Basic Rifle Marksmanship (BRM) on first qualification attempt (7.0 pts).
  • Land Navigation Perfection: Pass Land Nav written exam on 1st attempt AND find ALL 7 targets on 1st round during Land Nav PE (10.0 pts).
  • First-Pass Skills: Pass Warrior Skills (10/14) and Hand Grenades (5/7) on initial attempt.
  • Leadership Standing: Earn "Proficient" or "Excellent" ratings in evaluated FTX leadership roles with zero negative SPOT reports.
★ Strategic Impact: Earning RECONDO grants a +5 Point BONUS under Policy Memo #20. This bonus can elevate a cadet into the top 10% nationally on the Officer Order of Merit List (OML) for Active Duty and top branch selection!

MS1 to MS4 ROTC Leadership Progression

Your journey through the Cardinal Battalion builds progressively toward commissioning as a Second Lieutenant (2LT) in the US Army.

  • MS1 Year (Foundations): Master military customs, drill & ceremony, basic land nav, and time management. Build your GPA early.
  • MS2 Year (Tactical Skills): Develop squad tactics awareness, map reading speed, physical fitness, and initial leadership roles.
  • MS3 Year (Advanced Leadership): Lead squads and platoons during labs, prepare for Advanced Camp evaluations under Memo #20, and aim for RECONDO.
  • CST Advanced Camp (Fort Knox, KY): 35-day evaluation testing fitness, marksmanship, land nav, tactical leadership, and resilience.
  • MS4 Year (Battalion Staff): Plan training, mentor junior cadets, complete branch commissioning requirements, and prepare for active/reserve service.

Land Navigation 101 Masterclass

1. Standard Military Map Colors (TC 3-25.26)

On a standard 1:50,000 military topographic map, colors identify natural and man-made features:

Black

Man-made features: buildings, surveyed points, railroads, and non-dirt roads.

Blue

Hydrography & water features: lakes, rivers, swamps, and intermittent streams.

Green

Vegetation with military significance: woods, forests, orchards, and jungles.

Brown / Red-Brown

Relief features and elevation: contour lines, spot elevations, and red-light readable roads.

Red

Major populated areas, main highways, and special highlight features.

White

Clear or open terrain: clearings, open fields, and areas lacking dense vegetation.

2. Reading Grid Coordinates ("Right Then Up")

Always read left-to-right along the horizontal axis first (easting), then bottom-to-top along the vertical axis (northing).

Visual: Reading "Right Then Up" in a Grid Square

3. Compass & Back Azimuth Formulas

To calculate a Back Azimuth (exact opposite direction to retrace your path back):

  • If your current azimuth is LESS than 180°: ADD 180°
  • If your current azimuth is GREATER than 180°: SUBTRACT 180°

Locating Positions: Intersection & Resection

When in the field, you will often need to locate distant unknown targets or fix your own position using a compass and map.

1. Intersection

Purpose: Finding the unknown grid location of a distant object (e.g., enemy bunker or distant tower) without traveling to it.

  1. Orient your map at Known Position A. Shoot a magnetic azimuth to the unknown object. Convert to grid azimuth.
  2. Draw a line along that grid azimuth on your map from Position A toward the target.
  3. Move to Known Position B (at least 90° angle away). Shoot a second azimuth to the same target and convert to grid azimuth.
  4. Draw the second azimuth line from Position B. Where the two lines intersect is the grid location of the target!
2. Resection

Purpose: Finding YOUR unknown current location when you are lost or unsure of your exact position.

  1. Orient your map to North. Identify 2 or 3 prominent distant landmarks on the terrain that you can also locate on your map.
  2. Shoot a magnetic azimuth from your position to Landmark 1. Convert to grid azimuth.
  3. Calculate the BACK AZIMUTH ($Azimuth \pm 180^\circ$). Draw this back azimuth line from Landmark 1 back toward yourself.
  4. Repeat for Landmark 2. Where these back azimuth lines intersect is YOUR current location!
Visual Diagram: Resection Line Intersections

Measuring Distance Along Curved Travel Paths / Routes

In actual tactical operations, roads, trails, and streams bend. Measuring straight-line distance with a protractor ruler gives an inaccurate distance for curved routes.

Step-by-Step Method Using a Paper Strip
  1. Take a straight strip of paper: Place the clean straight edge at your starting point A along the trail.
  2. Mark start & first bend: Make a tick mark on the paper strip at Start Point A and at the first bend in the trail/road.
  3. Pivot along bends: Pivot the paper strip at the first bend so the straight edge aligns with the next straight section of the trail. Make a tick mark at the second bend.
  4. Repeat to Destination: Continue pivoting and marking tick marks along every curve until reaching End Point B. The distance between your Start Mark and Final Mark equals the total curved route distance.
  5. Align with Map Scale Bar: Place your paper strip against the graphic bar scale at the bottom of your map (check whether scale is in Meters, Kilometers, or Miles). Read the distance directly.
Visual: Measuring Route Curves with Paper Strip

Types of Terrain Slopes (Contour Line Density)

Contour line spacing tells you the steepness and profile of the ground before you walk on it:

Uniform Slope

Contour lines are evenly spaced. Ground rises at a constant steady angle.

Convex Slope

Contour lines closer at bottom, wider at top. Steep at bottom, gentle near crest.

Concave Slope

Contour lines wider at bottom, closer at top. Gentle at bottom, steep near crest.

The "Hand Method" for Terrain Features (EducationConnection Mnemonic)

A quick field trick taught in land navigation is to hold out a loose fist. Your hand naturally forms the contour shapes of major and minor terrain features:

Visual: Knuckles & Fingers as Terrain Features
  • Knuckles = Hills: Individual high points on terrain.
  • Dip between Knuckles = Saddle: A low point on a ridge line between two hills.
  • Line across Knuckles = Ridge: A continuous line of connected high ground.
  • Finger Extension Downward = Spur: High ground extending outward ($U$-shaped contours point down).
  • Grooves between Fingers = Draw: Drainage course between high ground ($V$-shaped contours point up).
  • Cupped Palm = Depression: Low area surrounded by higher ground.

All 10 Military Terrain Features (Contour Signatures)

TC 3-25.26 classifies terrain features into 5 Major, 3 Minor, and 2 Supplementary categories.

Major Feature
1. Hill

An area of high ground. Ground slopes down in all directions from the crest.

Signature: Closed concentric contour rings.
Major Feature
2. Ridge

A line of high ground with height variations along its crest. Slopes down in sideways directions.

Signature: Elongated U/V shape closed rings.
Major Feature
3. Valley

A stretch of low land between hills or mountains. High ground on three sides, typically contains water.

Signature: V-shaped contours pointing UPSTREAM (high ground).
Major Feature
4. Saddle

A dip or low point between two higher peaks along a ridge crest line.

Signature: Hourglass / figure-8 contour shape between 2 peaks.
Major Feature
5. Depression

A low point in the ground or sinkhole surrounded by higher ground in all directions.

Signature: Closed contour rings with inward tick marks (hachures).
Minor Feature
6. Draw

A small stream course/drainage line with no flat valley floor. Ground rises sharply on both sides.

Signature: V-shaped contour lines pointing TOWARD high ground.
Minor Feature
7. Spur

A short, continuous sloping tongue of high ground extending outward from a hill or ridge side.

Signature: U/V-shaped contour lines pointing AWAY from high ground.
Minor Feature
8. Cliff

A vertical or near-vertical drop. Contour lines converge into a single line or show tick marks pointing down.

Signature: Merged contour lines or hachured drop line.
Supplementary
9. Cut

A man-made feature where high ground was cut through to construct a level road or railroad track.

Signature: Hachures along road pointing INWARD toward roadbed.
Supplementary
10. Fill

A man-made elevated embankment built up to carry a road across a low valley or marsh ground.

Signature: Hachures along road pointing OUTWARD away from roadbed.

Test Your Knowledge: Land Nav Quiz

Answer the practical questions below to verify your mastery of military map reading and navigation concepts.

1. When reading grid coordinates on a 1:50,000 military map, what is the fundamental rule?
A) Read Left then Down
B) Read Right then Up
C) Read Top then Left
✓ Correct! Always move Right along the horizontal axis, then Up along the vertical axis.
2. What color represents vegetation with military significance (e.g., woods or forests) on a military map?
A) Brown
B) Green
C) Black
✓ Correct! Green represents woods and dense vegetation. White indicates clearings or open fields.
3. What procedure is used to find YOUR OWN unknown position by shooting azimuths to 2 known landmarks?
A) Intersection
B) Resection
C) Dead Reckoning
✓ Correct! Resection finds YOUR location using back azimuths from 2+ known landmarks. Intersection locates an unknown distant target.
4. You shoot an azimuth of 230° to a tower. What is the Back Azimuth?
A) 410°
B) 50°
C) 130°
✓ Correct! Since 230° is greater than 180°, subtract 180°: 230 - 180 = 50°.
5. Contour lines forming U/V shapes pointing AWAY from high ground represent which terrain feature?
A) Draw
B) Spur
C) Valley
✓ Correct! Spur contour lines point DOWN/AWAY from high ground. Draw lines point UP toward high ground.
